Usually dated from the late 1930s to the mid-1940s, the Golden Age is when American magazine science fiction grew
up, above all in *Astounding* under John W. Campbell, who took over in October 1937 [1]. Many date its real start to
the summer of 1939, when A. E. van Vogt, Isaac Asimov and Robert A. Heinlein all appeared in *Astounding* [1]. Its
stories believed in scientific progress and favoured problem-solving heroes [1].

A fanzine editor, Peter Graham, summed up the nostalgia: "The Golden Age of Science Fiction is twelve" [1].

## Why it matters
The era that produced the robot stories, Foundation and the writers who defined the genre for decades.
