John W. Campbell Jr. was born in Newark, New Jersey, on 8 June 1910 and died on 11 July 1971 [1]. His first
published story, "When the Atoms Failed", appeared in *Amazing Stories* in January 1930; he wrote space opera under
his own name and moodier stories as Don A. Stuart [1]. His novella *Who Goes There?* (1938) became the films *The
Thing from Another World* (1951) and *The Thing* (1982 and 2011) [1].

He became editor of *Astounding* in late 1937 and kept the job until his death [1]. He demanded logic and real
science, and discovered or shaped Isaac Asimov, Robert A. Heinlein and A. E. van Vogt; in Asimov's words, Campbell
"found magazine science fiction childish, and he made it adult" [1][2].
