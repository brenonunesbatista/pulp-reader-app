Herbert George Wells was born on 21 September 1866 in Bromley, Kent, and died in London on 13 August 1946 [1].
Between 1895 and 1901 he wrote the books that defined modern science fiction: *The Time Machine*, *The Island of
Doctor Moreau*, *The Invisible Man*, *The War of the Worlds* and *The First Men in the Moon* [1]. He has been called
the "father of science fiction" alongside Jules Verne and Hugo Gernsback, and Brian Aldiss called him the "Shakespeare
of science fiction" [1].

His reach went far beyond the genre: George Orwell wrote that "the minds of all of us … would be perceptibly
different if Wells had never existed" [1]. When Hugo Gernsback launched *Amazing Stories* in 1926, a Wells story was
in the very first issue, and Wells reprints ran in it month after month [2].

## Why it matters
Almost every road in this Atlas starts here: time travel, alien invasion and the atomic bomb all entered fiction
through Wells.
