Frank R. Paul was born on 18 April 1884 in Radkersburg, Austria-Hungary, and died on 29 June 1963 in Teaneck, New
Jersey [1]. Hugo Gernsback hired him in 1914 to illustrate *The Electrical Experimenter*, and in April 1926 he painted
the cover of the first issue of *Amazing Stories* [1]. He painted 38 covers for *Amazing Stories* up to June 1929
and about 103 for Gernsback's *Wonder Stories* magazines [1].

His huge machines, robots and spaceships shaped how readers imagined the future; Arthur C. Clarke credited Paul's
November 1928 *Amazing* cover, showing Jupiter, with drawing him to the genre [1]. He was the guest of honor at the
first World Science Fiction Convention in 1939 and illustrated *Marvel Comics* #1 that same year [1].

## Why it matters
The pulp cover is an art form of its own, and Paul invented its look.
